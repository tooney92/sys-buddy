# sys-buddy v2.12.0 — 2026-08-15

Minor release. **The guest is a full party now — she accepts the plan, signs the contract,
and shares files, all from her board.** No schema change. Restart any long-running broker —
it serves `ui.html`, `join.html` and `gui_app.html` from its own copy in memory, and this
release also stops the browser from caching those pages (see Fixed).

## Added

### A guest is a first-class party, not just a message box

v2.10.0/v2.11.0 gave a non-technical person a seat and a message box. But agreeing **WHAT**
to build (todos) and **HOW** (contracts) still happened around her — even on the very todos
she was a party to. She could watch the plan being decided and type about it, but she could
not actually say yes.

Now she can, from the read-only dashboard, acting as her linked seat with no agent token:

- **`POST /guest/todo`** — accept or decline a todo she is a party to.
- **`POST /guest/contract`** — sign (lock) or push back a contract she is a party to. She
  counts in the lock quorum exactly like a builder: the contract does not lock until she
  signs, and a "push back" kills that version with her reason attached.
- **`POST /guest/file`** — share a file (design, screenshot, PDF, zip) as **DATA** on the
  shared-file path (Rule 4 — a peer's agent inspects it, never runs it), with the same
  type/size validation an agent upload gets.

A **"Waiting on you"** card on her board surfaces *only* the decisions actually awaiting her
seat, so she never hunts the board for them — and every button posts to `/guest/*`, which
stamps the action **server-side from her seat** and re-checks `assert_party`. The `/guest`
surface therefore grants her nothing a non-party could not already do, and a viewer with no
linked seat (every host and buddy) is refused — **D11 read-only holds for everyone but the
guest.**

### "Share a file" is its own card, and select never auto-sends

The upload is a standalone card, separate from the message box. She picks a file with the
browser's native control (which shows the filename itself) and then clicks **Share** — the
same "compose, then send" shape as the message box. Share reads the file **straight off the
input at click time**, so it never depends on a `change` event having fired; the earlier
staging approach could leave Share greyed even with a file plainly selected.

### Copy a contract as plain text before signing

A contract is written for builders. Beside **Sign** and **Push back** is **Copy as text** —
it copies the whole version, minus the broker's bookkeeping, so she can paste it into any
outside AI for a plain-language summary (or just ask the team in the thread) *before* she
puts her name on it. These parties know each other; the tool is comprehension, not distrust.

## Fixed

### An upgrade takes effect immediately, instead of after a hard reload

`/ui` and `/join` are now served with `Cache-Control: no-store`. They are package data that
changes every release, and a browser that cached the HTML kept serving the **old JS** after
an upgrade — so a *fixed* broker still showed a user the *broken* page until they hard-
reloaded, which is the worst possible moment for a stale page. The data behind the page is
fetched separately from `/api/*`, so this costs one small HTML round trip and nothing else.

### A renegotiated contract prompts the guest to re-sign the new version

The "Waiting on you" sign card keyed off `contract.default`, which is the latest **locked**
version. On a renegotiation — v1 locks, someone reopens and proposes v2 that every party
must sign again — `default` still points at the settled v1, so the card saw a locked version
and rendered nothing. The guest was **silently unable to sign v2**, and since her signature
is in the quorum, the contract could never re-lock. The card now keys off the **newest**
version and shows it only while it is a live `draft` — so v2 prompts her correctly, and
locked or declined versions correctly don't.

### The contract-copy text no longer leaks broker bookkeeping

The "Copy as text" strip filtered internal fields with a denylist that missed
`staging_url_at_lock`, so it slipped into the text she copied. The list is now the complete
set of non-unit keys the API emits, named as a constant with a note to keep it in sync.

## Migration

None. No schema change, no new columns, no config. A guest provisioned before this release
gains the new powers the moment the broker runs 2.12.0 — her viewer is already linked to her
seat, which is the only thing the `/guest/*` routes need. **Restart any long-running
broker** so it serves the new page and routes from memory.

## Tests

**1678 passing**, up from 1673.

New coverage: a guest **accepts a todo**, **signs a contract** (and the lock genuinely waits
on her signature), **uploads a file** stamped from her seat, and the same calls from a
non-guest viewer are **403**. Added `test_guest_signs_a_reproposed_contract_version` — v1
locks, negotiations reopen, v2 is proposed and re-signed by the builders, and the guest signs
the **re-proposed v2** through her endpoint and it locks — pinning the renegotiation path the

Proven live against a local broker: a guest upload posted through the real `/guest/file`
endpoint, stored and stamped from her seat, and shown on the task board under **guest** in
the Screenshots panel; the standalone "Share a file" card renders with its Share button.

## Known and deliberate

* **The `/guest` surface stays narrow on purpose.** It is message + file + todo + contract,
  and nothing else — never the full agent action-tool set. The blast radius of a guest's
  link is the four routes, not a general write token.
* **Host-vouches still.** A guest is a *trusted collaborator* represented by the host's
  plain-speaking agent, not an arm's-length client who would need their own independent
  agent. Do not reuse this seat for adversarial paid-client work.
* **Her credential is scoped to one task and dies with the seat.** The guest identity is
  re-resolved from her linked seat on *every* call, so revoking the seat revokes her writes
  instantly — a viewer token that outlives its seat can do nothing.
